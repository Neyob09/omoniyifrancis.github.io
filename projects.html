Featured Dissertation Project

Cardiovascular Disease, Physical Activity, and Mortality

A data-informed mathematical modeling and longitudinal survival-analysis framework

My dissertation investigates how time-varying physical activity levels modify the relationship between cardiovascular and mortality among older adults (United States as a case study). The project combines repeated longitudinal observations with mathematical modeling to connect empirically estimated mortality patterns with population-level disease dynamics.

Research Problem

Physical activity, cardiovascular disease, and mortality evolve over time. The project examines how changes in activity and cardiovascular status relate to mortality risk and how those relationships can be incorporated into a mechanistic mathematical model.

Data

Longitudinal observations from the Health and Retirement Study are used to examine adults age 50 and older across repeated study waves.

Statistical Methods

Time-varying Cox proportional hazards models, survival estimation, proportional-hazards diagnostics, interaction analysis, and longitudinal data management.

Mathematical Methods

Compartmental modeling, systems of ordinary differential equations, dynamical systems, numerical simulation, and parameter-informed mortality functions.

Mathematical Biology

Social Influence and Obesity Dynamics

This project develops a compartmental mathematical model of obesity dynamics that incorporates social influence, multiple obesity-severity classes, and relapse. The framework is designed to capture transitions among weight-status groups and investigate how behavioral and population-level processes may influence long-term obesity dynamics.

Methods

Compartmental modeling, nonlinear differential equations, equilibrium and stability analysis, numerical simulation, and intervention analysis.

Outcome

The work resulted in a peer-reviewed publication in Franklin Open.

Chronic Disease Modeling

Mathematical Modeling of Type 2 Diabetes

This project examines the dynamics of Type 2 diabetes using a mathematical modeling framework that considers behavioral and environmental influences on disease progression.

Methods

Differential-equation modeling, qualitative analysis, numerical simulation, and investigation of model parameters associated with disease progression.

Outcome

The project resulted in a peer-reviewed publication in the Journal of the Nigerian Society of Physical Sciences.

Optimal Control

Hepatitis B Transmission and Control

This work uses mathematical modeling to examine hepatitis B transmission dynamics and investigate strategies for reducing disease burden through mathematical control interventions.

Methods

Compartmental epidemic modeling, differential equations, stability analysis, optimal control theory, and numerical simulation.

Outcome

The work resulted in a peer-reviewed publication and has also formed part of my research presentations on infectious-disease modeling and optimal control.

Health Data Science

Longitudinal Health and Survival Analysis

In addition to mathematical modeling, I work with longitudinal health data to study time-to-event outcomes, changing exposures, missing observations, and epidemiologic risk factors. These projects have strengthened my interest in connecting statistical evidence with mathematical models of population health.

Methods

Survival analysis, time-varying covariates, longitudinal data restructuring, missing-data methods, inverse probability weighting, statistical programming, and data visualization.
