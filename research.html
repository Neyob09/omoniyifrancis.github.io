Research

My research combines mathematical modeling, dynamical systems, scientific computing, and longitudinal statistical analysis to investigate problems in mathematical biology and population health.

Dissertation Research

Mathematical Modeling of Cardiovascular Disease–Mortality Dynamics: A Data-Informed Framework with Physical Activity Effect Modification

My dissertation investigates the relationships among physical activity, cardiovascular disease, and mortality using both longitudinal statistical analysis and mathematical modeling. The empirical component uses repeated observations from the Health and Retirement Study to examine how physical activity and cardiovascular disease are associated with mortality over time among adults age 50 and older.

The statistical component uses survival-analysis methods, including time-varying Cox proportional hazards models, to examine mortality risk while accounting for changes in physical activity and health status over time. I am also interested in effect modification, particularly whether the relationship between physical activity and mortality differs according to cardiovascular disease status.

The mathematical component develops a compartmental framework in which individuals are classified according to cardiovascular disease status and level of physical activity. Transitions between activity levels, cardiovascular disease states, and mortality are represented through systems of differential equations.

A central goal of the project is to connect empirical hazard estimates from longitudinal data with the transition and mortality structure of the mathematical model. This creates a framework in which observed population data can inform mechanistic models of disease and mortality dynamics.

Research Questions

Physical Activity and Mortality

How is time-varying physical activity associated with all-cause mortality among older adults?

Cardiovascular Disease and Effect Modification

Does cardiovascular disease status modify the association between physical activity and mortality?

Population Heterogeneity

How do mortality patterns and physical activity associations vary across demographic groups?

Data-Informed Mathematical Modeling

How can estimates obtained from longitudinal survival analysis be incorporated into a dynamical systems model describing cardiovascular disease, physical activity, and mortality?

Mathematical Modeling Framework

The mathematical model consists of activity-specific compartments for individuals without cardiovascular disease, corresponding compartments for individuals with cardiovascular disease, and a mortality compartment.

Individuals may transition between physical activity levels, develop cardiovascular disease, or move into the mortality compartment. The framework allows mortality rates and other transition processes to depend on physical activity and cardiovascular disease status.

I am studying both autonomous and time-dependent formulations of the model. The model can be used to investigate long-term behavior, compare scenarios, study intervention effects, and examine how changes in physical activity may alter cardiovascular and mortality outcomes at the population level.

Methods

Survival Analysis

Cox proportional hazards models, time-varying covariates, proportional-hazards diagnostics, interaction models, and survival estimation.

Dynamical Systems

Ordinary differential equations, compartmental modeling, equilibrium analysis, stability analysis, and time-dependent systems.

Scientific Computing

Numerical simulation, parameter exploration, visualization, sensitivity analysis, and computational implementation of mathematical models.

Health Data Analysis

Longitudinal data management, repeated-measures structures, mortality outcomes, epidemiologic covariates, and reproducible statistical analysis.

Broader Research Interests

My broader research interests include mathematical biology, epidemiological modeling, nonlinear dynamical systems, optimal control, scientific computing, survival analysis, and the use of mathematical and statistical methods to study public-health problems.

I am particularly interested in problems where mathematical models can be strengthened by empirical data and where statistical analysis can be interpreted through a mechanistic modeling framework.

Additional Research Areas

Obesity Dynamics

Mathematical modeling of obesity dynamics, including social influence, multiple severity classes, relapse, and intervention strategies.

Diabetes Modeling

Mathematical investigation of Type 2 diabetes dynamics under environmental and behavioral pressures.

Infectious Disease Modeling

Compartmental modeling of infectious-disease dynamics, including hepatitis B transmission and control.

Longitudinal Health Research

Statistical analysis of repeated health observations, survival outcomes, missing data, and time-varying exposures in population-health studies.
